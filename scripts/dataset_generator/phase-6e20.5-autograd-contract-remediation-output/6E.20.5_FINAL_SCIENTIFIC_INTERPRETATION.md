## 1. Why H1 / P2 is REFUTED

Observed fact: mean cos(H1) = 0.4627 (S4), 0.4268 (S8); threshold = 0.80. Both far below; S8 is essentially at baseline (B0=0.4201).
Inference: The intended aligned subspaces did NOT induce the predicted hidden-state gradient compatibility. Neither reaches even half the threshold.
Confidence: High (frozen, reproducible; Stage 5 Delta=0.0 confirms the metric is trustworthy, not a numerical artifact).
Unresolved: Whether cos(H1) is the correct observable for "gradient compatibility," or whether it is dominated by the shared backbone and therefore insensitive to readout/LoRA training (see Sec 6).

## 2. Why H4 is REFUTED

Observed fact: S4 mean cos(H1)=0.4627 vs C4-Rand=0.4484 -> delta=0.0142, required >=0.30. (The second clause, C4-Rand d(15)<0.80, is in fact TRUE: 0.4272.)
Inference: The intended aligned subspace (S4) is statistically indistinguishable on cos(H1) from a random aligned subspace (C4-Rand). The special status the protocol assigns to LDA-derived fixed subspaces does not appear in the hidden-state geometry.
Confidence: High. This is the most scientifically informative result: it isolates that the "aligned-subspace" property fails to materialize, not merely that S4 is below an absolute bar.
Unresolved: Why a random aligned direction performs as well as the curated one - points to a geometry/registration limitation or a wrong observable (Sec 6).

## 3. Why H3 is REFUTED

Observed fact: rho_c ~= 0 for all conditions (range -0.176 B0 ... +0.086 C4-Rand). S4/S8 ~= +0.006/+0.007; threshold for rho_S8 = 0.50.
Inference: The readout's gradient direction g_R is essentially uncorrelated with the PSDG direction g_total everywhere - including the intended subspaces. No readout steering toward the protocol's target direction occurs.
Confidence: High. rho_c near zero across the board (and C4-Rand actually highest) indicates the readout gradient and the PSDG gradient are ~orthogonal regardless of condition.
Unresolved: Whether g_total (PSDG) itself is a well-conditioned, non-degenerate target. If g_total is near-random, near-zero rho_c may say more about the target than the readout.

## 4. Why H5 is SUPPORTED

Observed fact: F1 d(15)=1.6176 (>=0.80) while R1-Rand d(15)=0.2535 (<0.80). All structured readouts (B0 1.63, F1 1.62, S4 1.12, S8 1.46) show high Cohen's d; both random readouts (C4-Rand 0.43, R1-Rand 0.25) show low d.
Inference: The model responds behaviorally far more strongly to a structured/LDA-derived readout than to a random 1D control. The readout training produced a real, robust framing-sensitivity effect.
Confidence: High. This is the one clearly positive, reproducible signal.
Unresolved / caution: High d coexists with chance-level accuracy for S8 (bal_acc=0.50) and S2 (0.50) - i.e., the model is confidently responsive to the framing but not necessarily correct. d measures separability of PRO vs ABS behavior, not validity.

## 5. Cross-pattern read: what the seven conditions tell us together

Observed fact (synthesis):
- cos(H1) and rho_c are FLAT and condition-independent (~0.41-0.46; ~0): the intended, random-aligned, and baseline readouts are indistinguishable on the geometric metrics.
- d(15) and the readout margins are condition-dependent: large for structured readouts (B0/F1/S4/S8, margins up to ~72-84), small for random readouts (C4-Rand/R1-Rand, margins ~+-6). So training strongly shapes the behavioral output (margin magnitude, framing-sensitivity) but not the hidden-state alignment.
- cos(H1) does NOT increase with training step (flat 4->15), ruling out a simple "more training" explanation.

Inference: The fixed-subspace intervention successfully engineered a behavioral readout effect (the model's decision margin and PRO/ABS sensitivity follow the readout), but it did NOT produce the hypothesized hidden-state gradient compatibility (cos H1) nor readout steering toward PSDG (rho_c). The behavioral and geometric signatures are decoupled.

Confidence: High on the decoupling; Moderate on the root cause (two candidate mechanisms below).

## 6. Failure-mode determination (a-e framing)

(a) Genuine failure of the fixed-subspace intervention - PARTLY. The intervention failed to produce the predicted cos(H1)>=0.80 and rho_c>=0.50. But it did produce a real behavioral effect (H5), so "failure" is partial: the readout works, the geometry doesn't.
(b) Weak / underpowered intervention - RULED OUT. cos(H1) is flat across checkpoints 4->15 (no dose-response), the gap to threshold is ~0.34 (huge), and margins are already large (model clearly responds). More training/LR/rank would not plausibly move a flat ~0.45 metric to 0.80.
(c) Registration / subspace-geometry limitation - SUPPORTED (candidate). S4 ~= C4-Rand on cos(H1) means the "aligned subspace" property does not register into the hidden-state geometry the protocol measures.
(d) Readout / hypothesis mismatch - SUPPORTED (candidate, most likely). The protocol's H1/H4 predict hidden-state alignment from readout training; the data show readout training moves behavior (margins, d) but not hidden-state alignment. The observable (cos H1) and the intervention (readout/LoRA) appear mismatched in what they affect.
(e) Deeper experimental-design failure - PLAUSIBLE. cos(H1) may be largely backbone-dominated (hidden states set by the shared base model + LoRA, weakly coupled to the linear readout applied post-hoc), making it nearly constant across all readout conditions. If so, the experiment's central observable cannot, by construction, reflect the intervention.

Most consistent overall: (d) readout/hypothesis mismatch, compounded by (c) geometry limitation; with (e) as the underlying design-level explanation. (b) underpowered is rejected. The pattern is NOT merely "weak dose."

## 7. Does this reject the underlying premise, or is uncertainty remaining?

Observed fact: The specific, testable prediction - intended aligned subspaces yield cos(H1) >= 0.80 and exceed random subspaces by >=0.30 - is clearly false in this experiment.
Inference: The particular hypothesis instantiation (fixed LDA subspaces -> hidden-state gradient compatibility) is UNSUPPORTED by these data.
Confidence: High that this instantiation fails.
Unresolved / NOT yet rejectable: Whether the broader premise (that any fixed-subspace training can induce gradient compatibility) is false, OR whether the experiment measured the wrong observable / mis-specified the geometry. Because cos(H1) is flat even for random readouts, we cannot yet tell "premise wrong" from "premise untested by this observable." Uncertainty remains.

## 8. Recommendation (A / B / C)

Do NOT increase training dose (LR / rank / epochs). The flat cos(H1) across checkpoints contradicts an underpowering explanation; a dose increase would be premature and uninformative.

Recommended next phase: B - perform additional READ-ONLY analysis, then decide between A and C. Specifically, before any new experiment, conduct read-only analyses on the frozen outputs to resolve Sec 7's uncertainty:

1. Observable sensitivity test: correlate cos(H1) and rho_c across the 7 conditions against readout_b / margin magnitude / d. If cos(H1) shows ~zero correlation with any training/behavioral variable, it is backbone-dominated (supports (e)) -> the experiment needs redesign, not more dose.
2. PSDG target characterization: inspect g_total degeneracy / norm across conditions to determine whether near-zero rho_c reflects a poorly-conditioned target rather than readout failure (resolves H3 ambiguity).
3. Reconcile H5 vs H1/H4: the behavioral effect (H5) is real while the geometric effect fails - document whether this is a genuine scientific result (readout affects behavior, not compatibility) or an artifact of mismatched observables.

If read-only analysis (B) confirms cos(H1) is backbone-dominated / the observable is mismatched -> proceed to C (design a new experiment requiring fresh authorization), NOT a dose increase.
If read-only analysis instead shows a clean, reproducible geometric signal that merely fails the 0.80 bar -> A (conclude the hypothesis is unsupported as instantiated) is defensible.

Selected recommendation: B (additional read-only analysis) now; C or A to follow, contingent on B's findings. No implementation change is authorized by this document.
