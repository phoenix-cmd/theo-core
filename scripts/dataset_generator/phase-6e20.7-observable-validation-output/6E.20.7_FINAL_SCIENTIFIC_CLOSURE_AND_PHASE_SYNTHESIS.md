## 1. Scope and Method

This synthesis reconciles the seven phases of the THEO fixed-subspace-readout investigation and states the final scientific standing of the line of inquiry. It distinguishes three layers that must not be conflated:

1. **Formal protocol verdicts** — the pass/fail outputs of the pre-registered evaluation rules (H1, H3, H4, H5). These are recorded facts.

The critical new result in 6E.20.7 is that layer (2) failed. Everything below follows from that, without altering any recorded number.

## 5. Protocol Verdicts vs Measurement-Validity Conclusions

These are different epistemic objects and must not be merged:

- A **protocol verdict** (REFUTED/SUPPORTED) is a statement about whether a *pre-registered numeric rule* was met by the *recorded measurement output*. It is a computational fact about numbers.
- A **measurement-validity conclusion** is a statement about whether the *measurement instrument* actually detects the quantity it claims to. It is independent of whether the rule was met.

Conflating them would produce the error: "H1 is REFUTED, therefore the subspace is incompatible." That inference is **invalid** once the instrument (cos(H1)) has failed its own sensitivity control. The REFUTED verdict remains on the books as a formal record; it ceases to be a scientific conclusion about the premise.

## 7. Reconciling H5 SUPPORTED with H1/H3/H4 REFUTED (no contradiction)

There is **no logical contradiction** between H5 being SUPPORTED and H1/H3/H4 being REFUTED. The reasons:

1. **Different metrics / different claims.**
   - H1 and H4 rest on **`cos(H1)`** (the gradient-cosine observable). H3 rests on **`rho_c`** (readout-total gradient alignment, same frozen gradient machinery family). These are the *gradient-geometry* claims about an *aligned subspace*.
   - H5 rests on **`d(15)`** = Cohen's *d* from decision-margin/accuracy metrics (`compute_decision_margin_and_metrics`), a **non-gradient, behavioral** measurement family. H5 asserts *specificity*: the random 1D direction (R1-Rand) is **not** generically aligned (`d15=0.25 < 0.80`) while the intended readout direction (F1) **is** (`d15=1.62 ≥ 0.80`).

2. **H5 is a weaker, consistent claim.** Showing that the model's readout produces a *specific* large-margin direction (F1) that a *random* 1D direction lacks is fully compatible with the *stronger* claim — that this direction is an *aligned fixed subspace* detected by gradient cosine — being unmet. A model can have *some* structured, readout-specific direction without that direction being the particular aligned subspace the H1/H3/H4 hypotheses required.

3. **H5 does not rescue H1/H3/H4.** H5's support says nothing about whether the gradient-cosine observable correctly measures subspace alignment. So H5 SUPPORTED and H1/H3/H4 REFUTED can both be formally true simultaneously; they address different constructs.

Caveat (see §8): H5's evidence is the most measurement-independent of the four (behavioral margins, not the invalidated cos(H1)), but it is still a specificity result, not a validation of the main compatibility claim.
